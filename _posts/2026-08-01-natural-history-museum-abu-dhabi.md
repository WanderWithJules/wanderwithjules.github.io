Some trips take weeks of planning.

This one took about five minutes.

It was a Friday evening in Dubai, and I was browsing through things to do over the weekend. The new Natural History Museum in Abu Dhabi had been popping up all over my social media, but I had been putting off visiting it, thinking I’d go some other time.

Then I thought...

**Why not tomorrow?**

I went ahead and booked my ticket without thinking it through.

- Museum admission: AED 70
- Immersive Theatre: AED 30

By the time Saturday morning rolled around, I found myself cruising down Sheikh Zayed Road, bound for Abu Dhabi, with a cup of karak tea in my hand and a completely open mind.

I wasn’t really sure what to expect. I simply hoped to stumble upon something fascinating, maybe catch a glimpse of some ancient fossils, and see where the day took me.

I couldn’t have been more wrong.

## First impressions

The drive into Saadiyat Cultural District already feels different.

Everything looks modern, clean and purpose-built, almost like you’re driving into a place designed around curiosity.

Then the museum appeared.

The building’s design really stood out to me. At first glance, it looks like huge rocks have been cut into perfect geometric shapes. It’s simple, yet really eye-catching, and seems to blend into the surroundings rather than overpower them.

As I walked towards the entrance, I noticed dinosaur skeletons sitting atop the welcome sign, catching my attention right from the start.

That should have been my first clue.

The real surprise was still waiting inside.

## Walking into the lobby... and stopping in my tracks

![The enormous dinosaur skeletons dominating the museum's entrance hall.](/assets/images/natural-history-museum/lobby.jpg.jpeg)

*The first thing that stopped me in my tracks. These incredible dinosaurs dominate the museum's entrance hall.*

The second security let me pass, I froze.

In the center of the lobby stood gigantic dinosaur skeletons that immediately transported me back in time.

Not one.

Several.

Towering over everyone walking underneath them.

Nothing could have prepared me for how enormous they were in person, no matter how many pictures I had seen online.

I spent a good ten minutes wandering around them, taking them in from every possible angle and looking up in complete awe...

Those claws, though!

They instantly took me back to watching the *Jurassic Park* films growing up, and I genuinely had chills running down my spine.

![Standing beneath one of the museum's giant dinosaur skeletons.](/assets/images/natural-history-museum/bear.jpg.jpeg)

*Standing beneath these giants made me realize just how enormous they really were.*

One fact that completely blew my mind was learning from the guide that what I was looking at was around 80% original fossil, with only about 20% carefully reconstructed to replace missing bones.

Millions of years pass.

Bones disappear.

Predators scatter remains.

Weather destroys them.

When you see these dinosaurs, it’s not just the bones that are impressive. It’s the story behind them.

Scientists spend years piecing together the fossils they discover and using science to reconstruct the missing parts. You’re not just looking at giant skeletons. You’re looking at decades of research, discoveries and a puzzle that has taken years to solve.

It honestly felt like standing underneath creatures that shouldn’t exist anymore.

And yet...

There they were.

## More than dinosaurs

I expected dinosaurs.

I didn’t expect to travel through the entire history of our planet.

The museum takes you on a journey from the birth of the universe all the way to Earth’s future, moving through ancient oceans, prehistoric life, early mammals, meteorites, minerals, evolution and the incredible biodiversity we have today.

Some of my favourite moments included:

Standing inches away from the enormous sabre-toothed cat skeleton was one of my favourite moments.

![Sabre-toothed cat skeleton.](/assets/images/natural-history-museum/sabre-tooth.jpg.jpeg)

*One of the museum's most impressive prehistoric predators.*

- Gazing up at a massive pair of jaws that completely dwarfed my head.
- Touching the bony frill of a Triceratops. As someone who grew up fascinated by dinosaurs and slightly obsessed with *Jurassic Park*, that was a proper goosebumps moment.
- Walking among fossil displays that are hundreds of millions of years old.
- Seeing ancient footprints preserved in rock.
- Realising just how much life existed on Earth long before humans ever did.

One of the most surreal moments came when I realised I could actually touch a piece of the Moon.

Not a replica.

An actual piece of the Moon.

I also got to touch several meteorites that had travelled through space before landing on Earth. Standing there with my hand on them, it honestly felt like I wasn’t just walking through a museum. I was travelling through time, history and even space.

There were moments when I completely forgot I was in Abu Dhabi.

It genuinely felt like stepping into another world.

## The Immersive Theatre

I paid an extra AED 30 to experience the Immersive Theatre, and it was worth every dirham.

It’s not just about sitting back and watching a documentary. You’re completely surrounded by the experience.

It genuinely feels like you’re travelling through space and time. The visuals wrap around you, making you forget you’re sitting in a theatre and making you feel like you’re part of the journey.

Without giving too much away, it’s one of those experiences that is much better when you don’t know exactly what’s coming.

Just sit back and enjoy it.

## My favourite part?

Oddly enough...

It wasn’t the biggest dinosaur.

It wasn’t the theatre.

It wasn’t even one specific exhibit.

It was the feeling.

That childlike sense of wonder that somehow disappears as we get older.

Walking underneath creatures that ruled the Earth millions of years before humans existed has a funny way of making your everyday worries seem very small.

You start thinking differently.

About time.

About nature.

About how tiny we really are in the grand story of this planet.

And somehow, that’s incredibly comforting.

## Would I recommend it?

Absolutely.

No matter who you’re with, whether it’s children, your partner or simply yourself, like I was on a whim one Saturday morning, you’ll find something interesting around every corner.

I spent far longer there than I expected.

When you visit, set aside at least three to four hours so you can take your time and properly explore everything. This isn’t the kind of museum you want to rush through. There is so much to see, and every gallery has something that makes you stop, look and wonder.

## Final thoughts

This wasn’t a trip I had planned.

There was no itinerary.

No checklist.

No months of anticipation.

Just a random Friday decision that turned into one of the most memorable day trips I’ve had in the UAE.

Sometimes the best adventures really do start with saying:

**“Why not?”**

So, if you’re in Dubai and wondering whether it’s worth making the drive to Abu Dhabi...

Trust me.

It is.

For AED 100, I travelled through 13.8 billion years of history, stood beneath giants that once ruled the Earth, touched a piece of the Moon, held meteorites that had travelled through space and walked away feeling like a curious child again.

Not bad for a trip I only decided to take the night before.

Until the next spontaneous adventure,

**Jules 🌍🚗**
