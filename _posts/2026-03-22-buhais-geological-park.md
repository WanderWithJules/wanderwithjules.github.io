I Went Looking for a Quiet Eid Escape... and Found an Ancient Sea Instead

Some people spend Eid hopping between family lunches.

Some book flights.

I drove to Sharjah to look at rocks.

Not exactly the most exciting answer when someone asks, "What are your plans for the holidays?"

But hear me out.

I'd heard about Buhais Geological Park a while ago, saved it on Google Maps like I do with hundreds of places I'll "definitely visit one day", and then completely forgot about it.

Until Eid.

Dubai was buzzing. Malls were overflowing, every café had a waiting list, and I just wanted somewhere... quieter.

So, I filled up the car, grabbed my sunglasses, put on a playlist I'd probably listened to a hundred times before, and pointed the Land Cruiser towards Sharjah.

About an hour later, I was sitting with a coffee, looking at hills that were once beneath an ancient sea.

Talk about changing your perspective.

**This Place Doesn't Try Too Hard**

That's probably what I liked most about Buhais.

It isn't flashy.

There's no giant sign screaming "Instagram me!"

No souvenir shops every ten metres.

No loud music.

It's just... peaceful.

The building almost disappears into the landscape. From some angles, it feels like the mountain simply decided to become a museum.

I ordered a coffee before going inside and sat on the terrace for a while.

![Terrace overlooking the ancient landscape at Buhais Geological Park](/assets/images/buhais-geological-park/Cafe.jpeg)

No phone.

No emails.

Just silence.

Well... silence, apart from a few families enjoying their Eid break and the occasional bird.

I realized I hadn't actually sat still like that for weeks.

**Then I Walked Inside...**

I'll be honest.

Geology has never been my thing.

At school, if someone mentioned sedimentary rocks, my brain immediately switched off.

So, I wasn't expecting much.

Five minutes later I was reading every single information board.

How did that happen?

Instead of throwing complicated science at you, the museum tells a story.

It starts with oceans.

Not deserts.

Oceans.

Standing there, looking out at dry rocky hills through the huge windows, it felt almost impossible to imagine that everything outside had once been underwater.

Yet the fossils sitting in front of me proved it.
![Ancient marine fossils at Buhais Geological Park](/assets/images/buhais-geological-park/Rock.jpeg)

Millions of years ago, fish, shellfish and strange-looking sea creatures lived exactly where I was standing.

That completely messed with my brain.

**My Favourite Moment Wasn't Inside a Display Case**

It was outside.

After finishing the exhibition, I walked back onto the terrace.

Now those hills looked completely different.

Before entering, I had just seen...

rocks.

Afterwards, I started noticing layers.

Colours.

Patterns.

The lines running through the mountains suddenly meant something.

It's funny how learning even a little bit changes what you see.

The landscape hadn't changed.

I had.

**Imagine Explaining This to Someone 20,000 Years Ago...**

One display really stuck with me.

It explained that during the last Ice Age, the Arabian Gulf wasn't even there.

The sea level was so low that what is now the Gulf was mostly dry land.

You could probably have walked across places that today are completely underwater.

Then, as the climate warmed, glaciers melted and the sea slowly filled the basin.

I've lived in the UAE for years.

I've looked at the Gulf countless times.

I'd never once stopped to think...

*"This sea is actually quite new."*

That little fact somehow fascinated me more than any fossil.

**Time Is Weird**

One room talked about events that happened 14 million years ago.

Another jumped back 50 million.

Then over 200 million.

Eventually I stopped trying to imagine the numbers.

Because you can't.

Our brains aren't built for that.

Meanwhile, I still complain when the traffic light stays red for two minutes.

Perspective is a funny thing.

**Was It Worth Going?**

Without a doubt.

Not because it's the biggest attraction in the UAE.

Not because it's packed with flashy technology.

Because it made me slow down.

It made me curious.

It made me notice things I'd normally drive straight past.

And honestly, that's becoming quite rare.

**If You're Planning a Visit...**

Go early if you can.

Take your time.

Don't rush through the galleries.

Grab a coffee afterwards and sit outside for a while.

The view is part of the experience.

You don't need to know anything about geology before you visit.

I certainly didn't.

You'll leave knowing a little more than when you arrived.

And that's kind of the whole point.

As I drove back to Dubai that afternoon, the city skyline slowly replaced the rocky hills in my rear-view mirror.

The traffic returned.

Just a tank of fuel, an open road, and somewhere that reminds you the world has been here long before us... and will be here long after we're gone.
