*October 2026 \| Saadiyat Cultural District, Abu Dhabi*

I've lived in the UAE for years, but there are still places that make me realise how much of its story I don't actually know.

Zayed National Museum was one of them.

I expected Sheikh Zayed, the formation of the UAE and the history most of us who live here are familiar with.

I wasn't expecting to be taken back hundreds of thousands of years.

And before I'd even properly started looking at the exhibits, I was distracted by the building itself.

![Inside Zayed National Museum](/assets/images/zayed-national-museum/Stairwell.jpeg)

## First, Look Up

The inside of Zayed National Museum is enormous.

Curves, ramps, huge columns and open spaces seem to overlap above you, and I found myself stopping several times just to look up.

It's one of those buildings where getting from one gallery to another becomes part of the experience.

The museum tells the story of the UAE from its earliest human history through to the country we know today. One of the things that really stayed with me was just how far back that story goes.

We're so used to thinking about the UAE in terms of pearl diving, oil and the formation of the country in 1971.

But people were living, travelling and trading across this land unimaginably long before any of that.

## The Boat I Wasn't Expecting

One of the exhibits I kept coming back to was the Magan Boat.

![Magan Boat at Zayed National Museum](/assets/images/zayed-national-museum/Magan Boat.jpeg)

It's huge.

And standing beside it, you start thinking about the people who crossed these waters thousands of years ago without engines, GPS or anything remotely resembling the boats we know today.

It is easy to look at the Gulf now and think of ports, container ships and airlines connecting the UAE to the rest of the world.

Those connections existed long before any of those things did.

They just looked very different.

## And Then, Sheikh Zayed

Of course, eventually the story becomes much more familiar.

Sheikh Zayed is at the heart of the museum, but I liked that his story isn't treated as though UAE history suddenly began with him.

Instead, you understand more of the land, the people and the traditions first, and then see the country that emerged from them.

![Sheikh Zayed photograph at Zayed National Museum](/assets/images/zayed-national-museum/SZ Photo.jpeg)

There are photographs, personal stories, objects and recordings throughout the museum, and this was the part where it became less about dates and artefacts for me.

I've spent such a large part of my life in the UAE that some of this history feels strangely personal, even though it isn't my own country's history.

You recognise places.

You recognise traditions.

You recognise the UAE your parents' generation arrived in, and then look outside at Abu Dhabi today and realise just how much happened in an incredibly short period of time.

## The Screen You Can't Really Ignore

And then there was this.

![Large immersive screen at Zayed National Museum](/assets/images/zayed-national-museum/Big Screen.jpeg)

A vast screen filled with the desert.

It sounds simple, but at that scale it completely changes the space around you.

You aren't looking at a little museum display explaining the landscape. You're standing in front of something that almost makes you feel surrounded by it.

That contrast runs through much of the museum.

Ancient objects sit alongside huge digital displays. History is told through artefacts, photographs, sound, film and architecture rather than endless walls of text.

Which, frankly, my feet appreciated.

## What I Liked Most

I thought I was going to Zayed National Museum mainly to learn more about Sheikh Zayed.

I left thinking much more about everything that came before him.

The ancient settlements. The sea routes. The people who crossed the desert. The communities that traded with places far beyond this region. The generations who lived here before skyscrapers, highways and air-conditioning made the landscape we know today possible.

And then Sheikh Zayed's story makes more sense because you've seen what came before.

That's what I liked most about the museum.

It doesn't just tell you how the UAE was formed.

It gives you some understanding of what it was formed from.

And after more than 20 years of calling the UAE home, I still walked out having learned something about the place I live in.

That's probably the best reason I can give you to go.
