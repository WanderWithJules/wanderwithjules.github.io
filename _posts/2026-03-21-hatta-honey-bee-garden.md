*Visited during the Eid Al Fitr holidays.*

Not every holiday has to involve an airport.

During the Eid Al Fitr break, I found myself looking for somewhere different to spend the day without leaving the UAE.

Hatta seemed like the perfect excuse for a road trip, and while planning the route, I came across Hatta Honey Bee Garden.

I thought I’d spend an hour or so there before exploring the rest of Hatta.

I couldn’t have been more wrong.

## The Drive to Hatta

One of my favourite things about Hatta is the drive itself.

Within an hour and a half, Dubai’s skyscrapers disappear behind you, replaced by winding roads, rugged mountains and landscapes that make you forget you’re still in the UAE.

By the time I reached Hatta Honey Bee Garden, the weather couldn’t have been better.

The mountains were peeking through the clouds, and everything felt calm.

It was the perfect start to the day.

## More Than Just a Honey Shop

I’ll admit, I didn’t know much about the place before arriving.

I assumed it would be a small honey farm where you taste a few different varieties, buy a jar or two, and leave.

Instead, I found an interactive experience where you actually learn how a hive works and, if you’re brave enough, get to handle one yourself.

That definitely wasn’t something I’d expected.

## Becoming a Beekeeper for the Day

The first step was getting suited up.

We were handed bright yellow protective beekeeping suits, gloves and hats with mesh veils covering our faces.

Standing in front of the mirrors, everyone looked equally amused.

It wasn’t exactly the most flattering outfit I’ve ever worn, but it certainly did the job.

## Opening the Hive

Outside, the beekeeper carefully opened one of the hives.

Thousands upon thousands of bees covered every wooden frame.

I expected chaos.

Instead, the hive was surprisingly calm.

The guide explained how smoke helps relax the bees by interrupting their alarm signals, allowing the frames to be removed safely without disturbing the colony too much.

Watching him lift each frame was fascinating.

Every inch seemed alive.

![A beekeeper opening a hive at Hatta Honey Bee Garden](/assets/images/hatta-honey-bee-garden/Hatta-Honey-Bee-Garden-beekeeper.jpeg)

## Then He Asked If Anyone Wanted to Hold It

I honestly don’t know what came over me.

One minute, I was happily watching from a safe distance.

The next, I had a frame containing thousands of live bees in my hands.

![Holding a frame covered with live bees at Hatta Honey Bee Garden](/assets/images/hatta-honey-bee-garden/Hatta-Honey-Bee-Garden holding frame.jpeg)

It was much heavier than I expected.

The bees continued crawling over the honeycomb as though I wasn’t even there.

Despite standing inches away from thousands of bees, I never felt unsafe.

The guides were calm and knowledgeable, and explained exactly what to do.

As long as you stay relaxed, the bees usually do too.

## Finding the Queen

The most interesting part was trying to find the queen bee.

She blends in remarkably well.

The guide pointed her out, and suddenly it became obvious.

She was larger than the worker bees, with a noticeably longer body, quietly moving across the frame while hundreds of workers surrounded her.

If nobody had shown me where to look, I would have missed her completely.

![The queen bee surrounded by worker bees at Hatta Honey Bee Garden](/assets/images/hatta-honey-bee-garden/Hatta-Honey-Bee-Garden Queen Bee.jpeg)

## A Tiny City That Never Sleeps

Before this visit, I never really thought about how organised a beehive actually is.

Every bee has a job.

Worker bees clean the hive, collect nectar, build honeycomb and care for young bees.

Drone bees exist mainly to mate with the queen.

And the queen?

Her primary role is laying eggs, sometimes thousands in a single day during peak season.

Standing there, watching thousands of bees moving with purpose, felt like observing a perfectly organised little city.

## Bee School

Inside the visitor centre are colourful displays explaining the different types of bees and how the colony functions.

It isn’t overly scientific or difficult to understand.

Whether you’re visiting with children or you’re simply curious like I was, it’s genuinely interesting.

## Honey Tasting

After meeting the bees, it was finally time to taste the end product.

The garden produces several varieties of honey, each depending on the flowers available throughout the year.

Some were light and delicate.

Others had much richer flavours than I expected.

It also gave me a new appreciation for why genuine local honey costs what it does.

After seeing how much work goes into producing it, every spoonful feels well earned.

## Is Hatta Honey Bee Garden Worth Visiting?

Without a doubt.

It’s one of those attractions that surprises you because it offers something completely different.

You don’t just look at bees through glass.

You step into their world.

You learn.

You ask questions.

And if you’re feeling adventurous, you leave having held a frame covered with thousands of bees.

Not many places can offer that.

## Planning Your Visit

Allow around two hours if you’re taking part in the beekeeping experience.

Wear comfortable clothes and closed shoes, although you’ll be provided with full protective equipment before entering the apiary.

If you’re visiting Hatta for the day, the garden pairs perfectly with attractions such as Hatta Dam, Hatta Heritage Village or simply enjoying a scenic drive through the mountains.

## Final Thoughts

Sometimes the best adventures aren’t the ones you’ve spent months planning.

They’re the ones that happen simply because you decide to try something different.

I arrived expecting to learn a little about honey.

I left with a much greater appreciation for bees, beekeeping and the incredible amount of teamwork happening inside every hive.

And yes...

Holding thousands of bees was a lot less terrifying than I imagined.

It’s still one of the most unexpected things I’ve done in the UAE.
